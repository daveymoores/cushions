## Article

SISU makes cushions in two sizes: a 50 × 50cm square and a 60 × 40cm rectangle. Between them they cover most sofas, armchairs and beds. This is how to choose between them.

### Which cushion size suits most sofas?

For most sofas, start with 50 × 50cm. It sits comfortably against the back of a standard sofa, it is large enough to lean into, and it doesn't take over the seat.

The 60 × 40cm is the supporting size. It is 10cm lower and 10cm wider than the square, so it sits closer to the seat, supports the small of the back, and can go in front of a square without hiding it.

If you are buying only one size, buy the square.

### How do I measure my sofa for cushions?

Two measurements tell you most of what you need.

Seat depth. Measure from the front edge of the seat to the back cushion. On a deep seat, a 50 × 50cm cushion fills the gap and makes the sofa easier to sit back in. On a shallow seat, a large cushion pushes you towards the front edge. One cushion per seat is usually enough, and a 60 × 40cm alone often works better than a square.

Back height. Measure from the seat to the top of the back. On a high-backed sofa, a square sits well below the top line and looks settled. On a low, modern sofa, a 50 × 50cm cushion can stand above the back. Some people like that; if you don't, the 60 × 40cm stays below it.

Arm height matters too. A square at each end, leaning against the arm, should look roughly level with it or a little above. If the arms are very low, the rectangle is the neater choice for the ends as well.

### How many cushions per seat?

Start with one cushion for each seat and adjust from there. A three-seater takes three cushions without any trouble. Add a 60 × 40cm in front if you want a fuller look.

The test is simple: you should be able to sit back without moving anything. If you have to lift a cushion every time you sit down, there is one too many.

For counts by sofa size and how to arrange them, see our guide on [how to style cushions on a sofa](/journal/how-to-style-cushions-on-a-sofa).

### Can I mix 50 × 50cm and 60 × 40cm cushions?

Yes. This is where the two sizes work best together.

Because the rectangle is 40cm tall, it covers the lower part of a square placed behind it and leaves the top showing. You see both fabrics, and the arrangement has depth without taking up more of the seat.

When you mix them, keep the pattern contrast in mind. A strong pattern behind and a quieter one in front reads clearly. Several SISU covers also have a plain reverse, so a cushion can be turned over to calm the arrangement down.

### Where does a 60 × 40cm cushion earn its place?

- **Lumbar support.** On a deep or soft-backed sofa, a rectangle at the small of the back makes sitting upright easier.
- **Armchairs.** A single 60 × 40cm fits most armchairs without crowding the seat, where a square can push you forward.
- **Beds.** In front of pillows, a rectangle adds colour without the height of another square.
- **Benches and window seats.** Long, low seating suits a long, low cushion.

### Does the insert change the size you need?

A cushion's size is the size of its cover. What makes it look that size is the filling.

Every 50 × 50cm SISU cover comes with a 55cm duck feather insert. The extra 5cm fills the corners and keeps the cushion looking full rather than flat. The 60 × 40cm covers also come with a duck feather insert [confirm: the insert size used in 60 × 40cm covers].

If you are filling covers from elsewhere, the same rule is useful: choose an insert a few centimetres larger than the cover.

Start with 50 × 50cm, one per seat. Add a 60 × 40cm when you want support, a lower line, or a second layer in front. You can see both sizes in the [cushion collection](/collections/cushions).
