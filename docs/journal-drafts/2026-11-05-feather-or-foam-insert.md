## Article

The insert decides how a cushion looks a month after you bring it home, more than the cover does. There are three common options: feather, polyester fibre and foam. SISU uses duck feather. This is how they compare, and why.

### What is the difference between feather, fibre and foam inserts?

**Feather** is soft and has some weight to it. It settles into the corners of a cover, gives when you lean on it and recovers when you plump it. A good feather insert lasts a long time. It does need plumping now and then.

**Polyester fibre**, sometimes sold as hollowfibre, is light and usually cheaper. It feels springy when new. Over time it tends to flatten and can form lumps, and once that happens plumping doesn't do much to bring it back.

**Foam** holds an exact shape and stays firm. That makes it right for seat pads, bench cushions and box cushions with straight sides. In a scatter cushion it tends to look stiff, because it doesn't slump or take the shape of someone leaning against it.

### Which insert is best for sofa cushions?

For scatter cushions on a sofa or bed, feather usually gives the best result. It looks full without looking rigid, and it can be brought back into shape in a few seconds.

Fibre is a reasonable choice if you need something light, or if you want to avoid animal products. Foam is the choice for anything you sit on directly.

### Why does SISU use duck feather?

Because it gives a cushion the shape we want it to have: full, soft at the edges, and able to recover after it has been leaned on all evening.

The inserts are filled with duck feather and made in the Netherlands. They are certified to DOWNPASS, an independent standard for the origin and traceability of down and feather. They are also OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 certified, which means they have been tested for harmful substances.

### Why is the insert bigger than the cover?

Every 50 × 50cm SISU cover comes with a 55cm insert. [confirm: the insert size used in 60 × 40cm covers]

The extra size is what keeps a cushion looking full. An insert the same size as its cover leaves the corners empty and the middle thin, and it looks deflated from the first day. A larger insert pushes gently against every edge of the cover, fills the corners, and holds its shape for longer.

If you are buying an insert for a cover from elsewhere, the same rule is useful: go a few centimetres larger than the cover.

### Do feather cushions need plumping?

Yes. That is the trade for the softness. Under weight, feathers move towards the edges and flatten in the middle.

Plumping takes a few seconds:

1. Hold the cushion by two opposite sides and push them together a few times, as if closing an accordion.
2. Turn it a quarter and do the same with the other two sides.
3. Give each face a firm pat to settle the feathers.

Once a day, or whenever you tidy the room, is enough. A cushion that has been plumped regularly holds its shape better between times. [confirm: whether SISU inserts can be washed, and if so how]

### Is a duck feather insert ethical?

Feather is an animal product, and that is a fair thing to weigh up.

What we can say is what the certification says. The inserts are certified to DOWNPASS, which covers where the feather comes from and how it is traced. Its full criteria are published on the DOWNPASS website, so you can judge them for yourself.

If you don't use animal products, a feather insert isn't the right choice for you. Every SISU cover closes with a concealed zip, so it takes any insert of the right size. [confirm: whether SISU sells covers without an insert]

### Are feather cushions suitable if you have allergies?

We don't describe our inserts as hypoallergenic.

The OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 certification means the insert has been tested for harmful substances. That is not the same as an allergy test. If you know you react to feather, choose a synthetic insert instead. If you aren't sure, ask your doctor.

### So which should you choose?

For a sofa, an armchair or a bed, choose feather, and choose it a little larger than the cover. Use foam for seat pads and anything you sit on. Use fibre if you need something light or want to avoid feather.
