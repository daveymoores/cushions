## Article

Jacquard is woven, not printed. The pattern is made by the threads themselves, which is why it wears well and why it needs a light hand when it gets marked. Every SISU cover carries the same care note: spot clean recommended. This is what that means in practice.

### Why spot clean a jacquard instead of washing it?

In a jacquard, threads float over and under each other to build the pattern. A full wash can loosen those floats, soften the surface unevenly and pull the cover out of shape, so it no longer fits snugly around the insert.

Covers are also made of more than one fabric. A face in one fibre, a back in another and a fringe can each react differently to water and heat. Some fibres, viscose among them, are weaker when wet. Spot cleaning treats only the mark and leaves the rest of the cover alone.

Before you start, check the fibre content on the product page. It tells you what the front, back and trim are made of.

### How do I spot clean a jacquard cushion cover?

1. **Act quickly.** A fresh spill is far easier to lift than a dry one.
2. **Take the insert out.** Every SISU cover has a concealed zip. Removing the feather insert keeps water away from the feathers, which take a long time to dry.
3. **Lift, then blot.** Scrape off anything solid with the edge of a spoon. Then press a clean white cloth onto the mark to draw out what you can. Work from the outside of the mark inwards so it doesn't spread.
4. **Test somewhere hidden.** Try your cleaning mix on the inside of the cover first, near the zip, and check that no colour comes off on the cloth.
5. **Use cold water and a little mild soap.** A drop of gentle, colourless detergent in cold water is enough. Dab it on with a cloth. Don't pour water onto the fabric.
6. **Rinse by blotting.** Use a second cloth dampened with plain water to lift the soap out.
7. **Dry flat and slowly.** Lay the cover flat, away from radiators and direct sun. Put the insert back only when the cover is completely dry.

### What should I avoid when cleaning a cushion cover?

- **Rubbing or scrubbing.** It pushes the mark deeper and can raise or fuzz the surface of the weave.
- **Hot water.** Heat can set some stains and shrink some fibres.
- **Bleach and strong stain removers.** They can lift colour as well as the stain.
- **Soaking.** It wets the whole cover and invites uneven shrinking.
- **Washing machines and tumble dryers.** They are hard on a jacquard face and on fringe, which can tangle and loosen.
- **Hairdryers and radiators.** Fast heat dries the edges first and tends to leave a ring.

### How do I keep a jacquard cover looking good day to day?

- **Vacuum gently.** An upholstery brush on a low setting lifts dust from the texture of the weave.
- **Turn it over.** Many SISU covers have a plain reverse. Turning a cushion now and then spreads the wear.
- **Watch the sun.** Strong direct sunlight fades most fabrics over time. A cushion that sits in a bright window every day will change faster than one that doesn't.
- **Look after the fringe.** Smooth it with your fingers if it tangles. If a thread comes loose, trim it with sharp scissors rather than pulling it.

### When should I leave a mark alone?

Sometimes the honest answer is to stop.

A faint mark on a busy pattern is often invisible from a step away. Repeated cleaning in one spot can leave a lighter patch or a ring that shows more than the original mark did. If a mark has faded and hasn't moved after one or two careful attempts, leave it.

A cushion that is used will show some signs of it. That is part of living with it.

### What are the limits of cleaning a deadstock fabric?

Deadstock is surplus fabric from the interiors industry: ends of rolls, over-orders and discontinued collections. That brings two practical limits.

The first is information. Surplus rolls don't always arrive with the full care details a newly woven fabric would carry, which is one more reason to start gently and test first.

The second is replacement. Each SISU design is cut from a finite roll, so once a fabric is used up there is no more of it. A cover that is badly damaged can't be remade in the same cloth. [confirm: whether SISU offers repairs, or a replacement cover while a fabric is still in stock]

Take the insert out, blot rather than rub, use cold water and mild soap, test first, dry flat. Most everyday marks come out with that. You can see each cover's fibre content on its page in the [cushion collection](/collections/cushions).
